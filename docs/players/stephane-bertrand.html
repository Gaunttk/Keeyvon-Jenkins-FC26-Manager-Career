Profile

Stephane grew up in Marseille's Le Panier district, where street football on cobbled, sloped alleys shaped a close, low center of gravity that coaches now point to as the source of his footwork. He joined Olympique de Marseille's academy at 9 and stayed until 14, when a philosophy shift toward taller, more physical wingers left him without a contract. Wrexham's French scout Marc Delacroix saw him at the same regional trial that produced Jules Collin, and both signed on 2026-08-15 in the window that also brought Germany's Nico Kopp. His five-star dribbling and a POT ceiling among the highest in the academy made him a name the coaching staff mentioned unprompted well before his sixteenth birthday, and the promotion to the senior squad — with an immediate loan to Carrarese Calcio to get first-team minutes his own dressing room couldn't offer yet — came with the club's 'Has Potential to be Special' scouting tag attached. His father Antoine, a dockworker at the Port of Marseille, has started rearranging his shift pattern around kickoff times in Italy instead of Wales.
