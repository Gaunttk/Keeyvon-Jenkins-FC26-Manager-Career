Profile

Tine's unlikely path to the Racecourse starts on a dirt pitch outside Port-Gentil, Gabon, where he was first noticed not by a scout but by a Wrexham content producer filming B-roll for a documentary about the club's global academy scouting trips — a fifteen-year-old outjumping and outmuscling boys three years older for every ball in a pickup match happening on the edge of the shoot. The footage got forwarded up the chain almost as a joke; nobody expected it to end with a signing. It did. Wrexham flew him to Wales for a two-week trial that turned into a four-year contract, and within his first month of first-team training, staff were already comparing his combination of raw power and finishing instinct to strikers a decade further into their careers. He arrives with almost no football pedigree on paper — no famous academy, no viral highlight reel before that one — and every bit of upside a club could hope to find for nothing. His signing bonus went straight into a savings account for his mother, who still runs a fish stall in Port-Gentil's central market and has not, as of this writing, agreed to stop.
