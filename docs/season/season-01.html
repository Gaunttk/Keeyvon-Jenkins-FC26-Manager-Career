Season in Review

Keeyvon Jenkins took charge in January 2025 with Wrexham mid-table in the Championship and no guarantee of anything. Eighteen months later, his first full season in the dugout ended with the title wrapped up in April with a game to spare, and a 5–2 send-off against Middlesbrough on the final day to make it official on paper: 112 points from 46 games, a +64 goal difference, and a 31-point gap to second-placed Ipswich — the kind of margin that turns a promotion race into a coronation. Thirty-six wins, four draws, six losses. A hundred and eleven goals scored, forty-seven conceded. Wrexham lost as many games in the entire season as some sides lose in six weeks.

Kieffer Moore's 38 goals across all competitions is the number the headlines will remember, but the deeper story is a squad that got stronger as the season wore on rather than coasting: José Luis Rodríguez matured into a genuine 19-goal, 10-assist threat from the left; a run of free-agent pickups in the second half of the campaign — Andrés Gómez, Brian Gutiérrez, Damián Bobadilla — walked straight into the side and started contributing within weeks; and by the closing weeks of the season, three more debutants (Milán Vitális, Vladyslav Veleten, Alan Minda) and a converted academy graduate in goal (Mason Webber, 17) were all getting first-team minutes in a title-winning squad. An FA Cup run to the Round of 16 — eliminated only on penalties by a Premier League side — added gloss to a season that, on the pitch, had almost nothing go wrong.

Whatever comes next in the Premier League, this is the season that will anchor the Jenkins era's opening chapter: the one where a club that spent decades rebuilding itself from non-league obscurity won a Championship title by the widest margin anyone in this pressroom can remember, and did it playing attacking, expansive football rather than grinding results out. Historic is an overused word in football. This year, it fits.
