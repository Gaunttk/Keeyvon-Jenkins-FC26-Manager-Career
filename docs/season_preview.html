The Board's Mandate

There is no version of a "survive and grow" mandate left to give — Wrexham won the league outright last May, in their first-ever Premier League season, and now carry a trophy into a campaign that will also be the club's first taste of continental football. The board's message this summer has been notably restrained given the achievement: no talk of retaining the title as an expectation, no inflated transfer war chest thrown at the market. Business so far has been almost entirely value-driven — headlined by a free-transfer signing rather than a marquee arrival — which tracks with a boardroom that built this rise on smart recruitment rather than outspending the division.

"We're not trying to replace anyone. We're trying to not be one bad tackle away from a problem in that part of the pitch."

— Keeyvon Jenkins, on the Callens signing

A mid-table target the summer after winning the league reads, on its face, as a step down in ambition. It isn't — it's an honest read of a schedule that now has three competitions in it instead of two, a squad that sent thirteen players out on loan for development, and a manager who has said plainly that he'd rather under-promise against a league that spent all summer studying how Wrexham beat them than pretend last season repeats itself by default. The Europa League target sets the real, longer horizon: not a one-year fluke defended and then lost, but a program that keeps climbing on its own terms.

Squad Breakdown — Champions, Thinner Than the Table Suggests

Thirty-eight names sit on the senior roster, but thirteen of them are out on loan this season — Amelia, Cadamarteri, Minda, Ortega, Cuenca, Soria, Lord, Macia, Webber, Kopp, Bianchi, Martini and Sandoval — which is the right call for most of their development, but it means the group actually training at the Racecourse is closer to twenty-five bodies. The spine that won the league is intact and, in most cases, still improving. The gaps are real, and they're concentrated at the two ends of the pitch: goalkeeping depth behind Okonkwo, left-back cover behind Doyle, and a striker department reduced to one fit senior No. 9 with Yacel Amrizi still working back from a torn quad.

Bottom line: this is not a squad that needs to spend big to compete on three fronts — it needs one more senior striker and a genuine left-back, ideally before either gap costs points in a season with no margin for a thin patch. The Callens business is the model going forward: value over headlines, four-year security over marquee fees. That approach built a Premier League title out of a Championship squad in two seasons. There's no reason to abandon it now that the trophy's actually in the cabinet.

"Winning It Once Was the Easy Part" — Keeyvon Jenkins on Defending a Title He Still Can't Quite Believe

Owen Meredith sits down with the Head Coach three months removed from the trophy lift, ahead of Wrexham's first Champions League campaign.

Three months on from Leeds at the Racecourse, and Keeyvon Jenkins still hasn't put the trophy anywhere permanent. It sits, for now, in a glass case just outside the first-team dressing room at Colliers Park, close enough that every player walks past it on the way to training. "That's deliberate," he tells me, when I ask. "I want them looking at it every single day and thinking about how hard it was to get, not how easy it'll be to keep."

Owen Meredith: Let's start with the target itself. Mid-table, for a defending champion. That's a lower bar than plenty of fans expected to hear.

Keeyvon Jenkins: I know how that sounds. But I've watched this league punish teams that repeat themselves for a living. Every club we beat last year spent the summer studying exactly how we beat them. We're not the surprise anymore — we're the target. Mid-table is the honest read of that, not a lack of ambition.

OM: And yet you're also about to play your first-ever Champions League match. That's not a small thing to fold into a "mid-table" season.

KJ: No, it isn't — which is exactly why I've said the league phase is the target there, not further. Three competitions is a different kind of season physically and mentally than anything this group has done. I'd rather be honest about that now than pretend we're built to chase four trophies in year one of European football.

We didn't get here by spending like the big clubs. We got here by being smarter about where the money goes than anyone expected.

— Keeyvon Jenkins

OM: The one signing so far is a free transfer — Yanis Callens, twenty years old, no fee at all. That's a curious first piece of business for a Premier League champion.

KJ: It's the only kind of business that's ever made sense for this club. We're not trying to replace anyone with Callens. We're trying to not be one bad tackle away from a problem in the middle of the pitch, in a season where we genuinely can't afford one. Free, four years, and he can play three positions — that's not a headline, but it's good business.

OM: Your squad review this summer flagged the striker position as thin with Amrizi still out. Is a forward still coming before the window shuts?

KJ: [pauses] I'll say what I've said internally — it's a real gap, and I'm not going to pretend it isn't. Whether we fill it with a transfer or fill it with patience and Yacel's recovery is still an open conversation with the board. I'd rather get the right one than the fast one.

OM: Last one. You told me last summer you never got to find out what you'd have been at this level as a player. A year on, with a Premier League medal, does that feeling change at all?

KJ: Some. Winning it answers a version of the question. But defending it is its own question, and I don't have the answer to that one yet — nobody at this club does. That's the whole job this season, if you want the honest version. Not the football. Whether we can stay hungry enough to deserve what's already in that glass case.

Training's called, and Jenkins is gone before I can ask a follow-up — same as last summer, same restlessness underneath the calm. Whatever this season turns out to be, it's clear he still isn't treating any of it as finished business.
