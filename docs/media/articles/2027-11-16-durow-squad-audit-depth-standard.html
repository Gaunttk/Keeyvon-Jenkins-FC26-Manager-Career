Borrowed Time: A Squad Audit Against the Standard You Set Me

Third in the table on the underlying numbers of a twelfth-placed side. Internal briefing to the head coach on who is being underused, who is being carried, and the positions where the squad fails the depth standard — including the two wings, which fail it in opposite directions.

ED
Earle Durow
Head of Recruitment & Squad Analysis, Wrexham AFC · Wrexham AFC (Internal) · Nov 16, 2027

Keeyvon — the international break gave me time to do the audit you asked for in the summer and never chased. I've split it into the three things you actually asked about: who isn't playing enough, who we're better with alongside whom, and whether the squad meets the standard you set me. You will not like the order I've put them in.

Before any of it: we are third on numbers that belong to a team in twelfth. Ten league matches, six wins, no draws, four defeats, eighteen points. Fourteen scored, fifteen conceded. Last season we scored seventy-nine and conceded twenty-four across thirty-eight. That is not a dip in form, it is a different football team wearing the same shirts.

One shot in four is going in. That is not a finishing coach, that is a coin. Aston Villa away, two goals from five shots. Crystal Palace away, four from nine. Bournemouth out-shot us twelve to one at the Racecourse and we are still third. When the conversion normalises — and it will — the table will come and find us, and no amount of what follows below will matter as much as getting the shot count up.

We are winning matches we are being out-played in, and calling it a title defence. I'd rather we called it borrowed time and spent it.

— ED

1. Who should be playing more

A word on method, because you'll ask. I can't get minutes out of the match reports, only who appeared. So I rate a player against his own team's average rating in the same match — that way a seven in the 0-4 at Leeds counts for more than a seven in the 4-1 at Palace, which is how it should be. Sixteen competitive matches; I've thrown out the three summer tournament games in Spain because they were twenty-man rating dumps against opposition that didn't turn up.

E. Dijkstra (RB/LB, 20) — 5 apps+0.20
C. Doyle (CB/LB, 23) — 10 apps+0.18

Jamal Belghazi. Eighteen years old, seven competitive appearances, and a Rotation squad role, while rating better than every outfielder in the building except Heaven and Miley. He and Heaven have started together eight times. That is eight times too few. I don't need him sold to me — I need you to stop treating a seventy-seven overall centre-half as a squad number because he's eighteen. Heaven is twenty and he's your best player.

Elijah Dijkstra. Five appearances. Small sample, and I'll say so plainly rather than have you find the hole yourself. But he's the only natural cover for Cacace, and Cacace is twenty-six with his scouting range closed — what he is now is what he will be at thirty. If Dijkstra isn't getting league minutes in a season where we're being out-shot every week, when is he?

Callum Doyle. Ten matches, consistently above the line, and used as the third centre-back behind a partnership that isn't outperforming him.

Miley I'd flag the other way before you do: he looks underused on raw totals, but he's played every single match available to him since he signed on the thirty-first of August. Twelve from twelve. Thirty-seven and a half million and he has not missed a minute I can find. He is not the problem.

2. Who should be playing less

Andrés Gómez. I'll take this one on the chin — the file on him was mine. Twelve competitive appearances, seven in the league, an average league rating of 5.67, and a mark of −0.79 against his own team in those league games. He is rating the better part of a goal below the ten men around him, week after week, wearing a Crucial squad role at twenty-four with no development range left. Every one of the ten worst-performing partnerships in this squad is Gómez and somebody, which is not a chemistry problem — it is one man and a list of victims. Sell him in January while somebody still believes the overall rating.

Thiago Pitarch. Eighteen appearances, no goals, one assist, and −0.25 against his own team. He is tagged an exciting prospect and I still think he'll be one. He is currently the third most-used outfielder in the squad on the strength of what he might become. Cups, not league.

Brian Gutiérrez. Fourteen appearances from attacking midfield. No goals. No assists. His ratings are fine — he isn't playing badly, he is producing nothing, which from that position is the same thing. Ngumoha has two and eight from wide. Sauer has six and two. Twenty-three, eighty overall, no range left: that is a player to sell at the top of his value, not one to build a second title defence around.

And while we're being honest about the expensive end of the pitch: Toni Fruk, one goal in eighteen appearances. Highest-rated player in the squad. I'm not proposing anything — I'm putting it in writing so neither of us pretends in March that we didn't see it in November.

3. The goalkeeper you haven't decided about

A. Kaczmarek (17)2 apps · both cup ties, one a rotated XI

The fair caveat first: Okonkwo started three of our four heaviest defeats, so the collapse in front of him is in his numbers. But he rated 5.5 against Liverpool and 5.4 against Leeds — bottom of the pitch both times. That is not a keeper dragged down by the ten in front of him. Klaverboer's profile is the exact inverse: 7.2 against Manchester City in the Community Shield, 7.1 in the goalless draw at Inter. High marks in matches we didn't win is what shot-stopping looks like on a sheet of paper.

Klaverboer is the number one. Okonkwo is the one to move. Kaczmarek — seventeen, potential into the high eighties, twelve point seven million of the board's money — has been fed two cup ties, one of them the rotated side that lost four-one to Newcastle. That is a baptism, not a verdict, and he needs league football inside eighteen months or we wasted the fee.

4. Combinations — and why you should distrust this section

You asked who we play best with together. The honest answer is that sixteen matches, no minutes data and no record of the starting shape can't support that question, and anyone who hands you a confident answer from this data is reading tea leaves. What I can do is strip out each player's own baseline and ask whether a pairing beats the sum of its two parts. Here is what survives that, at seven to eleven matches a pairing — treat all of it as a hint, not a finding:

Pairings That Exceed Their Own Baselines
Doyle + Amrizi (7)+0.32 · +1.00 goal difference/game
Sauer + Miley (11)+0.20
Doyle + Miley (8)+0.20 · +0.38 goal difference/game
Miley + Ngumoha (11)+0.18
Belghazi + Mokio (7)+0.15

The one pattern I'd act on: Miley is in three of the top five, with both wingers and with the left-sided centre-half. That is exactly what you'd expect from a deep distributor whose passing turns Ngumoha and Sauer's isolation into service — and it is also exactly what you'd expect if he is simply very good and the pairing effect is an illusion. It's cheap to act on either way, because he already plays every week.

On raw weight rather than interaction, the spine reads Heaven with Miley, Doyle with Miley, Heaven with Cacace, Heaven with Doyle. Those are your best eleven, not your best chemistry.

What would make this answerable: record the starting eleven and the shape for every match. One extra field in the log. By March you'd have partnership data instead of my hedging, and I'd stop writing paragraphs like this one.

5. The depth standard — position by position

Your instruction was: an exciting-prospect ceiling at every position, a showing-great-potential ceiling as the clear backup, and nobody in the building below that. Two problems before the table.

First, the game closes a player's potential range once he's fully scouted, so every established senior — Cacace, Doyle, Bobadilla, Gutiérrez, Fruk, Gómez, Okonkwo, Klaverboer, Belghazi, Barbieri, Amrizi — reads blank. That isn't a gap in my work, it's the scouting screen closing. For those I've used what they are now and what tier the club still shows on them.

Second, and you should sit with this: we do not have a single player at eighty-five overall. Fruk at eighty-three is the ceiling of this squad. So the standard you set me is entirely a question about projection, and half the answers to it are fifteen and sixteen year olds.

Third, and this is the one that moves two verdicts: we play Sauer at right wing and Ngumoha at left. Sauer is a left winger by trade and by every position listed against his name. That reframes both flanks. The right isn't vacant — it is occupied by a man playing off his own side, and doing it for six goals, which is a better return than his rating suggests. The left is the one with the hole: Ngumoha starts it, and behind him there is Gutiérrez, who is a number ten we have listed on the wing, and then nothing. Soria, Ortega and Sandoval are all out on loan. I had the two positions the wrong way round in my own head until I checked the team sheets, so I'll say it plainly: left wing is one injury from a problem, and right wing is a compromise that is working.

6. What I'd do, in order

One. Sign a striker with an eighty-five-to-ninety ceiling in January, and spend whatever it costs. We are the reigning champions scoring one goal and change per match. Our top scorer is a seventy-three overall nineteen-year-old — Chido Obi, eight goals, and he has earned the shirt on merit, not on projection. Our highest-rated forward has one. Drouet in the academy is a genuine eighty-four-to-ninety, and he is sixteen and fifty-three overall; he is a 2031 answer to a 2028 question. This is the only gap I'd break the transfer record for twice.

Two. Settle the goalkeeper, because it fails your standard outright. Klaverboer is the number one. Sell Okonkwo. Kaczmarek takes the cups now and starts the season after next. Promote Mika Horn from the academy as the backup with a real ceiling. And be honest about the hoard — five keepers contracted at the club and two more coming behind them is not depth, it's indecision with a wage bill. Kopp and Webber should be sold from their loans.

Three. Sell Gómez — but line up the cover first. He is a backup, not a starter, so moving him costs us nothing in the eleven, which makes the decision easier than I wrote it a page ago. What it leaves behind is Bakke at eighteen and sixty-six, and Amrizi filling in off the front. That is not a Champions League right wing. Before we sell, find out whether the Minda loan at Real Betis carries a recall — twenty-four, seventy-five, right-sided, already ours — and failing that, Faure at Bristol City. Bertrand comes back in July 2028 and solves both flanks at once: a natural right winger frees Sauer back to his own side and finally gives Ngumoha a partner.

Four. Decide about Gutiérrez. Twenty-three, eighty, no range left, no goal contributions in fourteen. Vargas — ninety-to-ninety-four — is back from Ipswich in July 2028 to take that shirt. Whether he's ready at nineteen I genuinely don't know; I have two ratings on him and both are from friendlies in Spain. But you don't get to hold a place open for a nineteen-year-old and a non-scorer at the same time.

Five. Fast-track Pedersen at left-back. Mokio is playing well and is a tier below what you asked for. Pedersen's eighty-two-to-ninety-two is the in-house answer; Jensen's range is too wide to plan around yet.

And the housekeeping. Forty-one senior names, seventeen of them out on loan, twenty-four actually available to you. Six strikers and five goalkeepers under contract. Your standard says quality everywhere and we have been answering it with volume. Cashing out the tail — Bianchi, Soria, Sandoval, Cadamarteri, Minda, none of whom project past the standard — pays for a good part of the striker.

The academy, for what it's worth, is the healthiest part of this club. Lien at fifteen with a ninety-two-to-ninety-four range is the most valuable thing we own and nobody outside the building knows his name yet. Besse, Pedersen, Drouet, Collin, Horn, Forster — every one of them clears the bar you set. In three years this squad looks after itself.

We just have to still be in this division when they arrive.

Mokio was my recommendation and he's earned it. Gómez was my recommendation too. I'd like the record to show I put both sentences in the same briefing.

— ED
