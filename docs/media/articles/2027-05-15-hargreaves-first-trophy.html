165 Years in the Making: Wrexham Finally Have Their First Major Trophy

Two months ago I wrote that Wrexham's cup run needed no borrowed history to make its point. Beating Manchester City 2-0 at Wembley to actually lift the thing has made that point for me.

Oliver Hargreaves
Senior Football Correspondent · Sky Sports · May 15, 2027

In March, writing about Wrexham's win over Arsenal in the FA Cup semi-final, I described a club playing "on a level its history gives no precedent for" and resisted reaching for a comparison to make the point land. Two months on, the point has landed on its own. Toni Fruk scored twice at Wembley on Saturday, Wrexham beat Manchester City 2-0, and a club that spent the 2010s in the National League now has a major domestic trophy in its cabinet for the first time in a documented history stretching back to 1864.

What made this final unusual was not that Wrexham won it — by full time, few neutrals watching would have called it an upset — but how they won it. City pressed them into repeated turnovers for the first twenty minutes, the kind of spell that has ended matches for lesser opponents this season. Wrexham conceded the territory, weathered it, and then simply scored when the opening came. That is not the profile of a plucky underdog riding a single golden cup run. It is the profile of a squad, built younger than almost any other in the competition, that has learned to solve a problem mid-match rather than be undone by it.

Credit belongs with the academy pipeline as much as with any individual performance. Ayden Heaven, Jorthy Mokio and Rio Ngumoha started a cup final at the national stadium against a Champions League regular's back line, and none of them looked remotely overawed by the occasion — because, under Keeyvon Jenkins, that stage stopped being unusual for Wrexham's young players a long time ago. It is the same recruitment and development story that has taken this club to the top of the Premier League table, arriving at its logical conclusion on the same weekend the league title is still, remarkably, not yet decided.

Clubs get one fairytale cup run, usually once a generation, usually while treading water everywhere else. Wrexham are having theirs while chasing the league title in the same season. I said in March I didn't have a comparison for that. I still don't — except now they've actually won something to go with it.

— Oliver Hargreaves, Sky Sports

The picture has already moved on since I filed the first draft of this column. Wrexham followed the Wembley win with a 3-2 victory away at Manchester City in the league that same day, leaving the two clubs level on 81 points with one match apiece to play. Wrexham's goal difference is well clear of City's, but it is not yet a mathematical formality — a City win paired with a Wrexham defeat on the final day would still hand City the title on points, goal difference or not. What is no longer in question is the cup sitting in the Racecourse trophy cabinet. Whether it is joined by a first Premier League title comes down to one more round of fixtures.
