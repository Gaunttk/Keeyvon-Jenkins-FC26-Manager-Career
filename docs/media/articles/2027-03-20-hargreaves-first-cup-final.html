A Fifth-Tier Club Fifteen Years Ago Is Ninety Minutes From an FA Cup Final

Wrexham's 2-0 win over Arsenal puts Keeyvon Jenkins one game from a first major domestic final in the club's history. That sentence alone is worth pausing on.

Oliver Hargreaves
Sky Sports · Mar 20, 2027

Wrexham's rise from the National League to the Premier League table they currently top has been documented so thoroughly, so repeatedly, by outlets on both sides of the Atlantic, that it has become easy to file each new chapter under the same headline and move on. A 2-0 win over Arsenal in an FA Cup semi-final should not be filed that way. This is the first time in the club's 165-year history that Wrexham have reached a major domestic cup final, and it happened against a club that, until this fixture list paired them together, most Wrexham supporters would have watched only on television.

The context matters. Arsenal arrived at the semi-final as a Premier League side with continental pedigree and a squad built for exactly this stage of a cup competition. Wrexham, for a half, looked like the smaller club the seeding suggested they were — patient, compact, waiting. Then Chido Obi and Rio Ngumoha, both products of a recruitment pipeline that has become the club's defining feature under Jenkins, scored the goals that ended the tie in the space of nine second-half minutes. "That tells you where this squad is now," Jenkins said afterward, and for once the standard post-match line felt understated rather than inflated.

There is a version of this column that reaches for the obvious comparison — another provincial club, another rapid rise, another cup run that briefly made the football world stop and look. I am going to resist it, because Wrexham's case does not need borrowed history to make its point. A club that was playing in the fifth tier of English football within living memory of most of its current first-team squad's careers is one result away from a major domestic final, achieved not through a single golden cup run insulated from the rest of the season but as a byproduct of the best campaign in the club's history running in parallel. That is not a romantic upset story. That is a football club operating, currently, on a level its history gives no precedent for.

Clubs get one fairytale cup run, usually once a generation, usually while treading water everywhere else. Wrexham are having theirs while leading the Premier League. I don't have a comparison for that, and I've been doing this a long time.

— Oliver Hargreaves, Sky Sports

None of this decides the final itself, and Jenkins was careful in the mixed zone not to let the achievement of reaching it overshadow the job still in front of his squad. But whatever happens next, Wrexham have already done something no version of this club has managed before them. The rest — an opponent still to be confirmed, a first major trophy still to be won — is, for once, a genuine bonus on top of a season that had already rewritten the club's record books before a ball was kicked at a neutral venue in March.
