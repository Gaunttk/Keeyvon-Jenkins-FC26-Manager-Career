Christmas Top, a Wobble, Four Clear: Wrexham's January Answered the Only Question Left

Six weeks ago I wrote that Wrexham's opening seventeen games already belonged in the history books regardless of how the season finished. January is the month that started answering how it finishes.

Oliver Hargreaves
Sky Sports · Jan 31, 2027

In December, writing about Wrexham sitting second after seventeen Premier League matches, I was careful to say the piece did not need the club to actually win the title to justify itself — that the campaign had already earned its place in the conversation about the great promoted-club seasons regardless of where May found them. Six weeks and nine matches later, I stand by every word of that caveat. I also want to note, because it would be dishonest not to, that Wrexham have spent the intervening month doing a very good impression of a side that intends to make the caveat unnecessary.

The month opened with the club going top of the Premier League for the first time in its history, a 2-1 win over West Ham on Christmas Day moving Jenkins' side to first on 39 points from 18 games. It did not stay simple. A 0-1 defeat at Brighton on January 2nd — Wrexham's first loss since a mid-December reverse against Manchester City — dropped them to third, and for a few days the familiar promoted-club script, the one where the fairytale runs out of road somewhere around the New Year, looked like it might be writing itself after all. It wasn't. Wrexham have not lost since.

What's changed most since December isn't the results — Wrexham were already winning most of their matches in the autumn — it's the manner. The 1-0 win over Chelsea on January 6th was, by Jenkins' own admission afterward, the first time in a long stretch his side had controlled a Premier League match through sustained possession rather than defending deep and hitting teams on the counter: 52 per cent of the ball to Chelsea's 48, five shots to two, 2.0 expected goals to 0.3. It is a small tactical wrinkle in isolation, but it matters for the same reason Alf Ramsey's Ipswich mattered in 1962 — a newly-promoted side that can only win one way is a side the rest of the division will eventually solve. A side that can win multiple ways is a much harder problem, and January suggests Jenkins now has more than one lever to pull.

Then there is the Carabao Cup run, which deserves its own sentence in this column regardless of how the semi-final ultimately resolves: three academy graduates — Nico Kopp, Jermaine Lord and Vittorio Martini — were promoted from the youth setup and given their senior debuts on the same afternoon Wrexham won 4-1 away at Chelsea in the first leg. Whatever happens in the second leg, a club fielding teenage debutants in a cup semi-final at Stamford Bridge and winning comfortably is not a club papering over thin depth. It is a club that trusts its pipeline enough to test it in the biggest games available.

Second place after seventeen games invited comparison to the great promoted-club campaigns. Top of the table, four points clear, in the last week of January, with a title in the division below the year before — that isn't comparison anymore. That's a case being made on its own terms.

— Oliver Hargreaves, Sky Sports

None of this guarantees anything, and I said as much in December for good reason — the resources arrayed against Wrexham for the remaining third of this season, Manchester City chief among them, have not gone anywhere. But the specific fear that hangs over every fast-rising promoted club, the one where the wheels come off the moment the fixtures stop being kind, got its first real test in early January and Wrexham answered it inside a week. That is not a guarantee of anything in May. It is, at minimum, evidence that whatever Jenkins has built at the Racecourse survives contact with a bad week — which is more than most clubs in this position have ever managed to prove.
