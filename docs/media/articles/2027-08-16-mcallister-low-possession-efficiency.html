The Numbers Behind Wrexham's Opening Fortnight: Losing the Ball, Winning the Game

A 1-0 defeat to Manchester City and a 2-0 win at Aston Villa look like unrelated results a week apart. The underlying data says they're the same team doing the same thing — just with a different finish at the end of it.

James McAllister
Tactical Analyst · The Athletic · Aug 16, 2027

It has been over five months since this column last ran a number through Wrexham's play, and in the gap between that piece — a look at the academy pipeline behind a fourth straight Youth Rush title — and this one, the club won the Premier League, lost a Community Shield final in the last minute, and opened a title defence with a 2-0 win at Villa Park. A lot has changed. One thing has not: this remains a team that is entirely comfortable not having the ball.

Start with the shape of the two matches on the record with full data attached. Against Manchester City at Wembley, Wrexham had 41% of possession and four shots to City's twelve, and lost by a single Erling Haaland finish in the 89th minute. Eight days later at Villa Park, the numbers were, if anything, more extreme — 37% possession, five shots to Aston Villa's one — and Wrexham won 2-0. Two matches, two very different results, and almost the identical territorial picture. The difference wasn't control of the ball. It was what happened in the moments Wrexham didn't have it.

What separated Villa Park from Wembley was almost entirely Chido Obi. Two touches in the box, two finishes, six minutes apart either side of the hour mark — that was the whole difference between a result that reopened questions about a pre-season slump and one that shut them down before the first international break. Obi is now four goals into his last five appearances for the club across two competitions, still only nineteen, and still a name most of the league only half-knows despite arriving with a "Showing Great Potential" scouting tag from Manchester United's academy. "He doesn't need twenty touches to hurt you," Jenkins said afterward. "He needs one, and we're seeing that now every time he starts." Jorthy Mokio and Toni Fruk, both credited with assists on the night, were the supply line — a teenage full-back and a converted forward-turned-No.10 doing the buildup work that the possession numbers say the rest of the team barely got to do at all.

Wrexham had one shot on target in ninety minutes against Aston Villa. It went in, and so did the other one. A team that can win a Premier League away fixture on five shots and 37% of the ball is not accidentally efficient — it is coached to be exactly this ruthless.

— James McAllister, The Athletic

The obvious caution here is sample size — two matches with full stat breakdowns is not a season, and the three group-stage wins in the European International Cup that preceded them came without the same detail attached. But the shape is consistent with what this column has tracked from this squad since the Championship days: a team built to sit deeper than its status suggests it should, absorb spells of pressure, and make the other side's mistakes count more than its own. Against City that approach conceded once, in the 89th minute, to arguably the best finisher on the planet. Against Villa it conceded nothing and scored twice. The question this data can't yet answer is what happens when a team with genuine top-four quality doesn't gift Wrexham the space Villa did in transition — and with Brentford at the Racecourse and then a trip to Anfield both on the horizon inside a fortnight, that answer is coming quickly.
