The Numbers Were Fine Until They Weren't: Reading Wrexham's Last Month Through One Red Card

Four matches, three wins, nineteen shots for and nineteen against — a perfectly balanced stretch of underlying data, right up until Brian Gutiérrez's 28th-minute dismissal turned one match into a outlier that drags the whole month's numbers down with it.

James McAllister
Tactical Analyst · The Athletic · Oct 17, 2027

Take the four matches Wrexham have played since this column last checked in — Newcastle at home, Legia Warszawa in the Champions League, Brighton away, Leeds United at home — and look only at shots for and against. Nineteen each. Not a close approximation, not a rounding coincidence: nineteen shots for Wrexham, nineteen shots against, across four matches against four very different opponents in two different competitions. That is about as balanced an underlying-numbers stretch as a data column ever gets handed, and for three of those four matches it translated into exactly what you'd expect from a balanced team: 1-0, 3-1, 3-1, all wins.

Then came Leeds, and the numbers stopped being balanced in about the time it takes to get shown a second yellow card.

Four Matches, By the Numbers
Newcastle (H, W 1–0)47% poss. · 5 shots · 2 on target
Legia Warszawa (H, W 3–1)47% poss. · 5 shots · 3 on target
Brighton (A, W 3–1)49% poss. · 5 shots · 4 on target
Leeds United (H, L 0–4)42% poss. · 4 shots · 0 on target

Wrexham didn't manage a single shot on target against Leeds — zero from four attempts, the first time that's happened to this side all season — and conceded six shots on target in the same ninety minutes, having allowed a combined ten across the previous three matches put together. Tackles told the same story in reverse: Wrexham made 18 in that match, more than in any other game this stretch, which isn't a sign of a team dominating the ball back, it's a sign of a team that spent an hour and change chasing it. Playing a man light does that to a defensive unit no matter how well-drilled it is; you simply cannot cover the pitch with ten players the way you can with eleven, and the shot-on-target column is where that shortfall shows up first.

None of this excuses the discipline that created the situation — a team doesn't choose to go down to ten men, but it does choose the tackle that gets a player booked twice in eight minutes, and that's squarely on the individual, not the system. What the numbers do argue is that the underlying shape Jenkins has built this squad around held up perfectly well in every match this month where it got to operate at full strength. Balanced shot counts, respectable possession numbers even away from home, a goals-for/against split that favoured Wrexham in three matches out of four. The Leeds scoreline is real and it goes in the record book at 0-4, but it's a numbers story about eleven versus ten more than it is a story about a system that stopped working. The system worked three times out of four this month. The one time it didn't, it wasn't really the system's fault.
