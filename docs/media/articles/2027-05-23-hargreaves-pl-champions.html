The Fastest Rise in Modern English Football History Just Reached the Top

Wrexham have won the Premier League less than a year and a half after leaving the Championship. There is no comparable case in the modern game — and I've spent all week looking for one.

Oliver Hargreaves
Senior Football Correspondent · Sky Sports · May 23, 2027

Eight days ago I wrote that Wrexham's FA Cup win had settled, on its own terms, the question of whether this club's rise needed a borrowed comparison to justify it. I said the trophy in the cabinet was proof enough. I did not expect to be revising that column's central claim inside a fortnight, but here we are: a 2-0 win over Leeds United on the final day has made Wrexham champions of the Premier League, and the scale of what that means has forced me back to the record books in a way the cup final did not.

Start with the timeline, because the timeline is the story. Wrexham were promoted from the Championship at the end of the 2025-26 season, their first campaign back in the second tier of English football after a rise from the National League that has already been chronicled at exhausting length elsewhere. This is their first Premier League season. Not their fifth. Not their tenth, consolidating gradually the way promoted clubs are supposed to, the way the whole received wisdom of English football insists a newly promoted side must — survive first, compete eventually, contend maybe a decade down the line if the recruitment holds and the luck breaks right. Wrexham have skipped every one of those stages and won the league outright in year one.

I don't say "no modern precedent" lightly on this network. Leicester City's 2015-16 title is the comparison everyone will reach for by instinct, and it deserves the instinct — 5,000-to-1 odds, a club that had been fighting relegation the season before, a story that rewired what anyone thought possible in this league. But Leicester had been an established Premier League club for two years before they won it. Wrexham have won it in their very first. There simply isn't a second example of a newly promoted English top-flight club going straight through to the title in living memory, and I've spent the better part of this week trying to find one that isn't this one.

Leicester rewrote what a mid-table club could dream of. Wrexham have rewritten what a promoted club is even allowed to expect of itself in year one. Those are not the same achievement, and I think history will eventually judge them separately — and judge this one as the stranger of the two.

— Oliver Hargreaves, Sky Sports

What makes it stranger still is the shape of the squad that did it — the same academy pipeline that produced the FA Cup final side started this match too, with Matthieu Brunel and Stephane Bertrand making their senior debuts in the 69th minute of the title-clinching match itself, on the pitch at the exact moment the club needed nothing further from them but the occasion demanded everything of the club's identity. This is not a title bought in a single window and defended competently. It is a title built, visibly, in front of us, by a manager and a recruitment department who kept promising that the kids would be ready when it mattered and then put them on the pitch, in the biggest games, to prove it. I don't know what Wrexham do with a target on their back next season — nobody does, including them. But this doesn't guarantee a dynasty. It has already earned the club a place in the conversation about what's possible that no amount of spending elsewhere in this league can buy back.
