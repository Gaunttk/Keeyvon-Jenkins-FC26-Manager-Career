Cole: "Outcoached" Isn't a Word You Get to Use Once and Move On From

Jenkins used it himself after Chelsea ended Wrexham's Carabao Cup run on penalties. Darren Cole thinks the manager was right to say it — and that it's the first real crack in a story that's had none.

Darren Cole
Television Pundit · Sky Sports (Studio Pundit) · Feb 4, 2027

"I've been the one on this panel saying, hang on, let's see what happens when it actually goes wrong for them. Monday night, it went wrong for them. And credit where it's due — Keeyvon Jenkins stood in front of the cameras afterward and said the word himself. Outcoached. His word, not mine. I'm not going to pretend I didn't enjoy hearing it, because I've taken enough abuse in this seat for saying Wrexham hadn't been tested yet. Monday, they were tested. And for the first time all season, they came up short."

That's Darren Cole's opening line on Sky Sports' Monday Night debate, and it's worth reproducing in full because it's the most honest version of the argument he's been circling for two seasons: that a team built this fast, on this much promoted-club momentum, was eventually going to meet a night where the model cracked — and Wrexham's Carabao Cup exit, a 4-4 aggregate defeat to Chelsea settled on penalties after a 0-3 home loss in the second leg, is the closest thing to that crack the club has produced in the Premier League era. Wrexham had won the first leg away at Stamford Bridge 4-1, with three teenage academy graduates thrown straight into their senior debuts. Three weeks later, Chelsea won the return 3-0, the tie went to penalties, and Chelsea went through to face Bournemouth in the final.

Cole's argument isn't that the exit undoes anything Wrexham have built — he's careful, more careful than his reputation suggests, to say that explicitly on air. "I'm not sat here saying this proves the whole thing's a house of cards, because it isn't, they're still top of the league." His actual point is narrower and, on the substance, harder to dismiss than his usual hot takes: that a club running this many teenagers through first-team minutes was always going to hit a night where squad depth and game management mattered more than talent, and Monday was that night. "You can't have it both ways. Everyone loves the story when a seventeen-year-old scores the winner at Anfield. Fair enough. But when the kids come up against a Chelsea side that's actually decided to take the tie seriously in the second leg, and it goes 3-0, that's the same story. That's what happens when your squad's this young in a two-legged knockout format. It's not a scandal. It's just a cost nobody wanted to talk about in December."

He used the word himself — outcoached. I've been saying for two years that sooner or later this squad would meet a night its depth couldn't cover. Monday was that night. Doesn't undo the league form. But it's real, and it happened, and it's on the record now.

— Darren Cole, Sky Sports

Whether the rest of the panel agrees is, predictably, a separate debate — the counter-argument, that a team fielding academy debutants in a semi-final at Stamford Bridge three weeks earlier was never the favourite in the return leg regardless of who was coaching it, got plenty of air time too. But Cole's larger point survives the pushback: for a season that has produced almost nothing but statement wins and record-book milestones, Monday was the first result that actually cost Wrexham something they wanted, against a big-club opponent, with the manager himself using the word "outcoached" in front of the cameras rather than leaving it to the pundits. Cole has been wrong about this club plenty of times already this season. On the narrower question of whether Monday was real, he isn't.
