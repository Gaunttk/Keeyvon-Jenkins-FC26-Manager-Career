Cole: "He's About to Find Out What 'Too Much' Actually Feels Like"

Darren Cole isn't questioning whether Keeyvon Jenkins can coach. He's questioning whether one man can run a Champions League campaign and a national team rebuild on the same calendar — and whether U.S. Soccer just handed him a trophy-or-you're-out clause to make sure everyone finds out fast.

Darren Cole
Television Pundit · Sky Sports (Studio Pundit) · Sep 29, 2027

"Let's be honest about what we're actually debating here, because it isn't whether Keeyvon Jenkins is a good coach. He's a good coach. He's won the Premier League. Nobody on this panel is arguing that. What I'm asking is a scheduling question and a focus question, and I don't think either one has a good answer yet. He's currently got a club in the Premier League, the Champions League, the Carabao Cup, and presumably the FA Cup once that starts up, and he's just added an entire international federation's rebuild on top of it. Something gives. It always does. I'd just like someone to tell me what, before it happens instead of after."

Cole's opening segment on Sky's Thursday panel was less a takedown than a warning shot — he was careful, more than once, to separate the question of Jenkins's competence from the question of his bandwidth. But he didn't let the federation off easily either. "Look at that clause. Win the Women's Intl. Cup in twelve months or you're gone. That's not a normal contract, that's a federation covering itself in public in case this doesn't work, which tells you they know exactly how big a swing this is. If they were confident, they wouldn't need the trapdoor built in from day one."

What made the segment land, for once, was that Cole didn't pretend the fan reaction was unanimous either way — he read out a handful of comments live, including one that just said "that was not expected" and another asking, half-joking, whether anyone actually follows this club for anything other than the drama at this point. "That's the honest reaction, isn't it," Cole said. "Nobody knows what to make of it yet, including me. I'm not sat here saying it's a disaster. I'm sat here saying show me the results before you tell me it's a masterstroke, because right now it's a press release and a very unforgiving clause, and that's all it is."

He built something real at that club by being relentlessly focused on one job. I want to see how that survives being cut in half.

— Darren Cole, Sky Sports

Cole closed, characteristically, by marking his own scorecard for later: he'll revisit the question, he said, whenever Wrexham next drop points during a USWNT window, and again whenever the international team's own results come in — whichever happens first. "I'm not going to sit here and pretend I've got a crystal ball on this one. I've been wrong about this manager before and I said so on this exact panel back in May. But this is a different kind of bet than anything he's made so far, and I don't think 'trust the process' covers you when the process is now two full-time jobs."
