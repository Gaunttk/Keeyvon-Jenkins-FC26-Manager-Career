One Goal, One Clean Sheet, One Point Closer to the Title

Kieffer Moore did what Kieffer Moore does just before the break, and this time Wrexham simply refused to give it back — seven shots to nil, and a fellow play-off contender sent home with nothing.

There was a version of this fixture where Swansea City, sat third and building a case of their own for automatic promotion, made a night of it at the Racecourse. That version never arrived. Kieffer Moore rose to meet a 44th-minute chance and did what he has made a habit of doing all season, and from there Wrexham simply declined to let their visitors back into it. Seven shots to Swansea's zero by full time — a defensive performance as much as an attacking one, and the kind of afternoon a title-chasing side needs more of down the stretch.

Swansea held the ball for the better part of the ninety — 58% to Wrexham's 42%, 226 passes to 133 — and it barely mattered. A back line marshaled by Cleworth and Doyle, deputizing again in a reshuffled defense, didn't concede a single shot on target all match, and Okonkwo's afternoon in goal was the definition of routine. Moore's goal came from Wrexham's more clinical spell either side of half-time, and having done his job he was withdrawn at the break for Amrizi, a substitution that has become almost procedural for a manager unwilling to risk his top scorer's fitness once the job is done.

Swansea passed it around for an hour and a half and never once tested Okonkwo. That's not a fluke at this point in the season — it's a pattern.

— Owen Meredith

A raft of second-half changes — Broadhead, Cadamarteri, Cacace and Sheaf all introduced together in the 78th minute — did nothing to disturb the scoreline, though Cadamarteri's evening ended with what looked like a knock in stoppage time, one the club will need to clarify in the coming days. None of it took the shine off a result that keeps Wrexham eight points clear of second-placed Ipswich with a game in hand on most of the chasing pack. The automatic promotion picture is starting to look less like a race and more like a formality — the kind of sentence this column has learned to write carefully, this season of all seasons.

Two Down, Three Up: Wrexham Stun Aston Villa in Stoppage Time

Boubacar Kamara and John McGinn had this tie put away inside twenty minutes. Wrexham spent the next seventy clawing it back, and Kieffer Moore finished the job with virtually the last kick of normal time.

Villa Park has a way of reminding visiting lower-division sides exactly what division gap they're bridging, and for twenty minutes on Sunday it did just that. Boubacar Kamara opened the scoring in the ninth minute, John McGinn doubled it in the twentieth, and a cup tie that Wrexham had traveled to the Midlands hoping merely to survive looked like it might turn into a rout instead. It did not. What followed was the most complete comeback this column has watched Jenkins's side produce all season.

Brian Gutiérrez pulled one back just before the half-hour, a finish that kept the deficit at a single goal rather than the two-plus it threatened to become, and Wrexham went in at the break still very much alive. Damián Bobadilla, deployed from the start in central midfield, leveled it just after the hour — his second goal in as many senior appearances since arriving as a free agent. Villa held 68% of the ball across the ninety and doubled Wrexham's shot count, and by most conventional measures should have seen the tie out comfortably. They didn't get the chance. Kieffer Moore, introduced by no substitution at all — he started and finished it — buried the winner in the final minute of stoppage time, sending the away end into the kind of noise reserved for results nobody saw coming.

This was backs-against-the-wall football from a club that spent most of the ninety minutes being told, statistically, that it didn't belong in this fixture. Wrexham simply declined to listen.

— Owen Meredith

The man of the match honor going to Kamara, on the losing side, tells its own story about how the ninety minutes actually played out on the pitch — Villa were the better side for long stretches and still find themselves out of the competition. That's the nature of knockout football, and it's the nature of this Wrexham side under Jenkins: outplayed, outpossessed, and somehow still standing at the final whistle with the result that matters. The draw for the next round awaits. For now, a club still finding its footing among England's bigger names has one more story to tell about the night it refused to be embarrassed at Villa Park.

No Style Points, Just Three More

Kieffer Moore's 49th-minute finish was the only goal of an afternoon Wrexham will not remember for how it looked. A week after Charlton exposed them, the response was not a statement — just a result.

Vicarage Road does not tend to produce style points, and Wrexham did not go looking for any. Four days removed from the worst defeat of their season, Jenkins's side arrived at Watford with a simpler brief than usual: don't lose this one too. They didn't. Whether that counts as progress or merely damage control depends on how generous you're feeling about a league leader that needed one goal in ninety-plus minutes to see off a mid-table Watford side.

Moore's goal, four minutes into the second half, was the difference — his kind of goal, the sort he has scored all season without much fuss attached to it. Around him, the platform was Ben Sheaf, whose afternoon in front of the back four earned him the armband's quiet praise and the Man of the Match nod, the kind of performance that doesn't make the highlight reel but keeps a nervy scoreline from becoming a nervier one. The Charlton hangover, if there was one, didn't show up on the scoreboard. It showed up in how little this Wrexham side needed to convince anyone of anything on a Wednesday afternoon in Hertfordshire — one goal, one clean sheet, three points, gone by five o'clock. Whether that's a title favorite quietly re-asserting control or a team that's started grinding rather than gliding is a question for the run-in. For now, the gap holds, and the Racecourse's ledger reads a little healthier than it did a week ago.

Charlton End Wrexham's Unbeaten Run With a Battering

Charlie Kelman scored twice before half-time and Joe Rankin-Costello added two more in stoppage time. In between, Wrexham found one goal and not much else. The first defeat since Ipswich in November arrived exactly as those tend to — all at once.

There is a version of this result that reads as an aberration — a team eleven points clear having an off night at a relegation-threatened side's place, the kind of afternoon every runaway leader is entitled to once a season. That is not quite what Saturday was. Charlton were sharper in behind Wrexham's back line all afternoon, and Charlie Kelman made them pay for it twice before the break, the second finish arriving barely eight minutes after the first.

Rodríguez Does It Twice as Wrexham Brush Portsmouth Aside

No drama, no scare, no late nerves — just José Luis Rodríguez scoring either side of the hour mark and a home crowd that barely had to hold its breath.

Some wins announce themselves. Others simply accumulate, one comfortable phase of possession after another, until the scoreboard reflects what had been obvious for an hour. Saturday's visit from bottom-half Portsmouth was the second kind. José Luis Rodríguez opened the scoring inside ten minutes and added a second on seventy-five, and in between Wrexham did what they have made a habit of doing to sides beneath them in the table: made the game look easy without ever needing it to be dramatic.

Eleven Points

A month of results I didn't think I'd get to write about this calmly, a cup tie that nearly humbled us, and a title race that doesn't feel much like a race anymore.

Five weeks since I last sat down to write one of these. In that stretch: Derby, Leicester twice, Norwich, QPR, Sheffield Wednesday, Millwall, Bristol City, Notts County, and tonight, Ipswich. Nine matches, eight wins, one draw I'd have to go back and check because I genuinely don't remember losing anything in that run. That's not a boast. It's just what the log says, and I've learned this season not to trust my own memory of a stretch of games over what's actually written down.

The signings are the story everyone outside this building wants to talk about, and fine — Gómez, Gutiérrez, Bobadilla, three free transfers, all three among the goals inside three weeks of arriving. I'll take the praise for the recruitment model when it's offered. But the version of this run that means more to me is the one nobody's writing headlines about: Damián played sixty-five minutes total before I trusted him with a Cup start, and he didn't blink. A back line of Thomason, James, and a heavily rotated group went to Notts County and got dragged into a 3-2 game we had no business making that close. We won it anyway. That's the thing I keep coming back to — not that we're winning, but that we're winning with different eleven names on the sheet depending on the week.

I told the board in December that depth wasn't a luxury for this squad, it was the whole plan. Notts County was the closest thing to a stress test that plan has had. It held. Barely, but it held.

Bristol City away was the one that actually had my heart in my throat — twice behind, twice level, and then Rodríguez finds a winner in the ninetieth minute like it's nothing. I've stopped being surprised by that kid. Tonight against Ipswich was the opposite kind of night: nervy, low-scoring, backs against the wall for large parts of it, and Dominic Hyam — not a name that makes headlines — was the reason it stayed 1-0 our way instead of turning into something else. One goal, a title-race billing plastered all over the league's own coverage before kickoff, and we found a way to be the team that didn't blink.

Seventy-nine points. Eleven clear of Ipswich, and I have a game in hand I haven't played yet. I'm not going to sit here and call this won — I said that about a one-point lead in January and I'd be a fool to say it about an eleven-point one in February with a third of the season still to come. But I'll admit something I wouldn't have said out loud a month ago: I'm starting to let myself imagine what the run-in actually looks like from here, instead of just surviving it one Saturday at a time.

— KJ

And when the board went up for four minutes of stoppage time with the score locked at 2-2, this had the shape of a hard-fought point rather than three — right up until José Luis Rodríguez found the game's final goal in the ninetieth minute.
